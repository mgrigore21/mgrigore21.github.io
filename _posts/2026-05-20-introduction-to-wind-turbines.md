## Why Wind Turbines?

Wind energy is one of the fastest-growing sources of electricity in the world. Wind turbines are at the heart of it — elegant machines that turn moving air into clean electricity at a scale that can power entire cities.

In this blog I'll be writing about all aspects of wind turbines: how they work, how they're built, how they're operated, and where the industry is heading.

## How Does a Wind Turbine Work?

At its core, a wind turbine is simple:

1. **Wind pushes the blades** — shaped like aircraft wings, they generate lift rather than drag, so the rotor spins efficiently.
2. **The rotor turns a generator** — either through a gearbox (which steps up speed) or directly in modern direct-drive turbines.
3. **The generator produces electricity** — conditioned by a power converter and stepped up by a transformer before feeding into the grid.

## The Betz Limit

No turbine can capture all of the wind's energy — if it did, the air would stop moving and there'd be nothing left to push the blades. The theoretical maximum any turbine can extract is **59.3%** of the kinetic energy in the wind. This is called the **Betz limit**. Modern turbines operate at around 45–50% aerodynamic efficiency, impressively close to this ceiling.

## Scale: From Rooftop to Offshore Giant

Wind turbines come in a wide range of sizes:

| Category | Rated Power | Typical Use |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Micro | < 10 kW | Remote cabins, boats |
| Small | 10–100 kW | Farms, small businesses |
| Utility onshore | 2–6 MW | Wind farms |
| Offshore | 5–20 MW | Offshore wind parks |

The largest turbines today have blades longer than 100 metres and towers taller than the Eiffel Tower.
