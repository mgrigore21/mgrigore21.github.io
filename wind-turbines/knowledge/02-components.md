# Wind Turbine Components

## Rotor & Blades

- Typically 3 blades for utility-scale turbines (balance of efficiency and structural load)
- Made from fiberglass-reinforced epoxy or carbon fiber
- Modern blades: 50–120 m long
- Pitch system rotates blades around their axis to control power and protect in high winds

## Hub

Connects the blades to the main shaft. Houses the pitch control system.

## Nacelle

The housing at the top of the tower containing:
- **Main shaft** — transfers rotational energy from rotor
- **Gearbox** — steps up rotational speed (some modern turbines are direct-drive, no gearbox)
- **Generator** — converts mechanical rotation to electricity (typically DFIG or PMG)
- **Yaw system** — rotates the entire nacelle to face into the wind
- **Cooling system** — manages heat from gearbox and generator
- **Brake system** — mechanical and aerodynamic braking

## Tower

- Tubular steel (most common), lattice, or concrete
- Height: 80–160 m onshore; taller towers access stronger, steadier winds
- Tower height is a key factor in energy yield

## Foundation

- **Onshore**: reinforced concrete spread or pile foundation
- **Offshore fixed**: monopile (most common), jacket, tripod, gravity-based
- **Offshore floating**: spar-buoy, semi-submersible, tension-leg platform

## Electrical Systems

- **Power converter**: conditions variable-frequency AC from generator to grid-frequency AC
- **Transformer**: steps up voltage for transmission
- **SCADA**: supervisory control and data acquisition — monitors and controls turbine remotely
