# Wind Turbine Aerodynamics

## How Blades Generate Force

Blades are airfoils — shaped like aircraft wings. When wind flows over them:
- **Lift** acts perpendicular to the wind direction → drives rotation
- **Drag** acts parallel to the wind direction → opposes rotation

Lift force is much larger than drag for well-designed blades, so the rotor spins efficiently.

## Betz Limit

The theoretical maximum efficiency a wind turbine can achieve is **59.3%** (Betz limit). This is a physical law — no turbine can extract more than 59.3% of the kinetic energy in wind. Modern turbines operate at 45–50% aerodynamic efficiency.

## Tip Speed Ratio (TSR)

TSR = blade tip speed / wind speed

- Optimal TSR for 3-blade turbines: ~7–8
- Too low → blades act like a solid wall, excess drag
- Too high → turbulence from one blade disrupts the next

## Pitch Control

The blade angle relative to the wind (pitch angle) is actively adjusted:
- **Below rated wind speed**: pitch optimized for maximum power capture
- **Above rated wind speed**: blades pitched to shed excess power, keeping output constant
- **Cut-out / storm**: blades feathered (parallel to wind) to stop rotor

## Power Curve

Defines turbine output as a function of wind speed:
- **Cut-in speed**: ~3 m/s — minimum wind to generate power
- **Rated speed**: ~12–15 m/s — reaches maximum (rated) power output
- **Cut-out speed**: ~25 m/s — turbine shuts down to prevent damage

## Wake Effect

Downstream turbines receive slower, more turbulent wind from upstream turbines. Wake losses in a wind farm typically reduce output by 5–20%. Wind farm layout optimization (spacing, stagger) mitigates this.
