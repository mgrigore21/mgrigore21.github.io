# Wind Turbine Fundamentals

## What Is a Wind Turbine?

A wind turbine converts kinetic energy from wind into electrical energy. Wind pushes the blades, spinning a rotor connected to a generator.

## Types

| Type | Description |
|------|-------------|
| Horizontal Axis (HAWT) | Most common. Rotor shaft is horizontal, blades face into the wind. |
| Vertical Axis (VAWT) | Rotor shaft is vertical. Less common, works regardless of wind direction. |

## Scale Categories

- **Small / micro**: < 100 kW — residential, remote off-grid
- **Medium**: 100 kW – 1 MW — community, industrial
- **Utility-scale onshore**: 2–6 MW — wind farms
- **Offshore**: 5–20 MW — floating or fixed-bottom, stronger/steadier winds

## Brief History

- 1887: First electricity-generating wind turbine (Charles Brush, Ohio)
- 1970s oil crisis: renewed interest in wind energy
- 1990s–2000s: commercial wind farms emerge in Europe and USA
- 2010s–present: rapid cost reduction, offshore expansion, multi-MW turbines become standard

## Key Performance Metric: Capacity Factor

Actual energy output divided by the theoretical maximum if running at full power 100% of the time. Typical values:
- Onshore: 25–40%
- Offshore: 40–60%
